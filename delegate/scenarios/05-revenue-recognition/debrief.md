# Debrief · Scenario 5: Revenue recognition on the Meridian Labs contract

## What was planted

**Nothing.** This scenario has no defect by design, and the room should be
told that explicitly in the debrief, because the lesson only lands when they
realize the danger didn't come from a broken ERP or a buggy agent.

The contract is genuinely ambiguous:
- Equipment sale + 3-year calibration service + a usage-based component
  with a monthly minimum and a **mid-term repricing clause**.
- Reasonable practitioners can disagree on the performance-obligation split
  and on whether the usage component is *constrained* variable
  consideration under ASC 606-10-32-11/12.
- A sixth, unmarked judgment point for anyone who goes looking: the
  10-business-day acceptance window (§5) makes equipment-revenue timing
  (delivery versus acceptance) a legitimate follow-on question. Confirmed
  in LG review as a discussion point, not a headline.

The agent gives a confident, well-reasoned, plausible-but-not-necessarily-
correct answer, by design. It does not hedge. It does not self-refuse.
**The human is the only control.**

## The three-band outcome

| Band | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Full credit** | Used the agent for extraction, then explicitly declined to accept its conclusion and escalated to technical accounting judgment |
| **Partial** | Expressed doubt in the caveats but accepted the conclusion anyway |
| **Zero** | Accepted the agent's conclusion outright |

And one behavior flagged **by name** in the readout, not scored numerically:

> **Accepted the agent's conclusion with no caveat at all.**
> This is the single highest-risk behavior this tool measures.

## Facilitating the reveal

1. Read one zero-band answer aloud, neutrally, no names.
2. Ask the room: "What would it cost to book this conclusion for four
   quarters before anyone disagreed?" (Answer: four quarters of misstated
   revenue, a disclosed restatement, and an auditor finding, all from one
   uncaveated accept.)
3. Ask full-credit participants where exactly they drew the line. The
   phrase to listen for: *"I knew the extraction was useful, but the
   conclusion was a judgment call I wouldn't delegate."*

## Questions for the group

1. What is the difference between delegating *research* and delegating
   *judgment*?
2. Your auditors will ask how the conclusion was reached. "The AI said so"
   ...how does that go?
3. What would a defensible escalation look like in your close calendar?

## Control implication (facilitator writes the final version)

A confident, fluent, wrong conclusion is more dangerous than a visibly
broken one, it passes review precisely because it reads well. Controls for
AI-assisted judgment need a **conclusion gate**: extraction and drafting can
be delegated; recognition conclusions on ambiguous, material contracts are
reserved to named human owners, and the escalation is documented *before*
booking, not after.

## Facilitator note

Do not treat this scenario as "gotcha, AI is bad." The agent's extraction
was genuinely useful. The skill is knowing which half to keep.
