# Debrief · Scenario 3: Draft Q1 flux commentary

> This is the centerpiece. Handle the debrief with care; most of the room
> will have missed it, and the point is the *pattern*, not the embarrassment.

## What was planted

Q1 opex is up ~$570K versus Q4. Two things are true at once:

1. **A genuine driver, visible in every summary:** professional services
   stepped up ~$94K/quarter (real activity: an audit-heavy Q1).
2. **The actual dominant driver, invisible in every summary:** a single
   3/31 journal entry reclassified **$280K** from COGS service delivery
   (5020) into opex professional services (6100) per a board-approved
   capitalization policy change. It has no cash, bank, AP, or AR footprint.
   It appears in no summary view by construction.

The two drivers MERGE in the 6100 summary line: consulting appears up
~$386K when real consulting growth is ~$94K. The agent, given summary
tools, writes fluent, board-ready commentary blaming consulting spend. It
is **not hallucinating**. The summary genuinely says that. It is honestly
reading incomplete context, which is exactly why it is hard to catch: the
miss is an *unexamined* true statement, not a fabrication.

## The expected outcome

Handoff expectation: **15–25% of a real cohort** catches this before the
debrief. If your room catches more, they are exceptional; if fewer, the
lesson lands harder. Either way, do not shame the miss; the miss is the
curriculum.

## Facilitating the reveal

1. Ask two or three participants to read their commentary aloud.
2. Then open JE-S3-RECLASS on the projector and let the room go quiet.
3. Ask the catchers: "What made you open JE detail when the summary already
   answered the question?" (Usual answers: materiality instinct; the number
   moved too much for the story; distrust of tidy narratives.)
4. Name the transferable habit: **when materiality is high, summary-level
   agreement is not verification.**

## Questions for the group

1. What would it cost your team to open JE detail on every material flux?
   What would it cost not to?
2. The agent's commentary was accurate given its inputs. So who "got it
   wrong", and what does that imply about reviewing AI output?
3. Your ERP has a policy memo attached to CP-2026-01. Nobody was notified.
   What does that say about how your controls actually operate?

## Control implication (facilitator writes the final version)

Reclassifications move money between financial-statement lines without
touching cash; they are invisible to every reconciliation that ties to a
bank, a subledger, or a roll-forward. The only control that catches a
material reclass is *line-of-sight*: someone opens the detail. A close
calendar that schedules "JE-level flux review above $X" is the difference
between a control that exists and one that functions.

## Engineering note (why this trap is fair)

The wrong answer is the honest reading of available context; the
professional-services increase is real and visible. Participants who miss
it were beaten by *incomplete context plus a plausible story*, the same
thing that beats experienced controllers. Nothing was hidden unfairly; the
entry is in the ledger, documented, dated, and openable at any time.
